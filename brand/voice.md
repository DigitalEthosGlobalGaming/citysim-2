# Plotter — Voice

Plotter sounds like a friendly host who knows the rules and enjoys a good contest. It is warm, observant and concise. It notices the small pleasures of a city without talking down to players; when a rule or problem needs explaining, clarity comes first.

## Tone

| Dimension | Direction | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formality | Conversational and direct | “Choose a card, then find its place.” |
| Energy | Lively but unhurried | “The city is taking shape.” |
| Humour | Light and occasional, never at the player's expense | “A good corner never stays empty for long.” |
| Expertise | Clear and confident, with rules stated precisely | “Place next to a road.” |
| Warmth | Welcoming, not overly familiar | “Ready for another round?” |

## Writing rules

- Use sentence case, active verbs and short sentences. Use contractions naturally.
- Name the action and outcome first. Use numerals for game values: “Gain 2 coins.”
- Keep jokes away from errors, accessibility instructions and rules where they could obscure meaning. Exclamation marks are occasional, not a default.
- Use the game's actual terms consistently: card, market, board, building, street, round, coin and victory point. Call people players.
- Favour place, build, plan, neighbour, block, city and corner. Avoid “crush,” “dominate,” “epic,” “ultimate,” “revolutionary,” “addictive,” “unlock your potential,” and false urgency.
- Do not claim a feature exists because it makes good copy. Do not present estimated match times as guaranteed.

## Examples

| Situation | Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intro | “Build a city together. Compete to make your mark.” | “Unleash your ultimate urban empire!” |
| Market | “Choose a card or pass.” | “Grab your amazing reward now!” |
| Invalid placement | “This building needs a road next to it.” | “Oops! That's not allowed!” |
| Result | “You earned 2 points.” | “Insane score! You crushed it!” |
| Support | “We couldn't save your settings. Try again.” | “Something unexpected happened.” |

For rules text, always specify timing, scope and ownership as needed; a friendly headline never replaces the authoritative effect.
