# Plotter — Positioning

**Status:** Working hypothesis; not validated by market or audience research.

## Category and audience

Plotter is a competitive, card-driven city-building game for s&box. People may compare it with relaxing placement puzzles, city-building toys and social tabletop games. The best first audience to test is players who enjoy building a place and competing over spatial choices with friends or AI opponents.

## The position

For players who enjoy approachable strategy and city building, Plotter is a competitive tabletop-style city builder where everyone shapes the same city while pursuing their own score. A shared card market, simultaneous placement and building interactions make the other players' choices part of your plan.

**Short version:** One shared city, several competing plans.

## Reasons to believe

1. Players buy building cards from the same market.
2. Everyone places roads and buildings on one shared map during the active phase.
3. Placement can depend on nearby buildings, roads and tags.
4. Players earn individual points over eight rounds.
5. Solo setup supports one to four AI opponents; multiplayer supports up to four human players, according to the current project documentation.

Check each claim against the shipping build before using it publicly. Match duration remains a target until tested.

## Boundaries

Lead with a friendly competition, not a promise of a peaceful sandbox or a destructive battle game. Don't imply that players have separate cities or that decorative traffic controls the economy. Don't claim exclusive ownership of shared city building: [Between Two Cities](https://store.stonemaiergames.com/products/between-two-cities-essential-edition) is one relevant example.

## Evidence and next decisions

See [competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.md) for adjacent games and sources. Observe first-time players' explanations and real match times, then revisit the position if they consistently value a different aspect of the game.
