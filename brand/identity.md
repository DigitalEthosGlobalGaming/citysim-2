# Visual Identity Direction — Working Draft

## The idea

Make the game feel like a beautifully made tabletop city that comes to life as people play. The brand should celebrate the pleasure of placing a little building, watching neighbourhoods take shape and noticing how another player's choices change your plans. It is warm and playful, with enough restraint to make the strategy feel rewarding. Plotter is the preferred working title, pending further availability checks.

## Logo

Use a distinctive, sturdy wordmark with the character of a printed board-game box. Explore custom letterforms and a small city motif built into the lettering or held beside it: for example, a street corner with two differently shaped buildings. It should work as a single-colour stamp, remain legible as a small game tile, and have a horizontal version for menus. Avoid generic skyline silhouettes, oversized glossy lettering, gradients, bevels and a building squeezed into every letter.

## Colour

Start with a limited, ink-like palette. These are starting values to test against the game art and for contrast, not locked production tokens.

| Role | Colour | Hex |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper and light surfaces | Warm paper | #F2E8D3 |
| Main text and outlines | Deep ink | #293A3B |
| Primary brand colour | Brick red | #B94F3E |
| Secondary colour | City blue | #557D88 |
| Small highlights | Mustard | #D5A83D |
| Landscape | Park green | #71855B |

Let warm paper and deep ink carry most of the interface. Use red for the main brand signature, blue and green for environmental variety, and mustard sparingly for emphasis. Reserve separate, tested player colours and pair them with shapes or labels so ownership never relies on colour alone.

## Typography

Explore a sturdy, characterful display face such as **Alfa Slab One** for short headlines and an accessible sans-serif such as **Atkinson Hyperlegible** for rules, labels and UI. Check font licences and in-engine rendering before committing. Use sentence case, generous spacing and a clear size hierarchy. Keep gameplay text straightforward and readable rather than imitating tiny print on a physical card.

## Illustration and city

Treat the board like a little built object: simple, recognisable building silhouettes, gently imperfect printed edges, carefully chosen shadows and visible roads. Show neighbourhoods forming through the contrast between homes, parks, shops and industry. Let small cars and street details reward a closer look without obscuring the strategic board. A consistent viewpoint, lighting and level of detail matter more than adding texture everywhere.

Textures should be quiet, with paper grain or ink variation visible at larger sizes but never behind small rules text. Avoid glossy gems, neon reward effects, oversized currency piles, exaggerated 3D buttons, noisy gradients and arbitrary decorative detail. Illustrations should describe actual buildings or game interactions rather than rely on generic generated city imagery.

## Principles

1. **Make every piece recognisable.** A card, building and board marker should be identifiable by silhouette and label before decorative detail.
2. **Show the shared city.** Lead key art and screenshots with several players' contributions forming one lively place.
3. **Make it feel made.** Use restrained paper, ink and cut-piece cues consistently; keep surfaces clean enough for comfortable play.
4. **Let the decisions breathe.** Clear hierarchy, calm backgrounds and explicit feedback give the competitive game room to shine.

## Applications

- **Game tile and store art:** A compact wordmark over a small, legible shared neighbourhood; one strong focal point and a few distinct pieces.
- **Main menu:** Frame the city like a tabletop scene, with a quiet paper panel for navigation and a clear primary action.
- **Cards:** A consistent printed structure, distinctive building illustrations and precise, readable rules. Preserve labels alongside icons.
- **In-game board and HUD:** The city is the hero; keep the HUD quiet, use high-contrast text and give player ownership clear redundant cues.
- **Community posts:** Use cropped board moments and short, specific captions about the choices players made; use the ink palette and wordmark consistently.

## Voice samples

- Pitch: Build a city together. Compete to make your mark.
- Market prompt: Pick a card. Find its place in the city.
- Placement feedback: Good neighbours. +2 points.

These are exploratory copy, not final taglines or a promise of specific mechanics.
