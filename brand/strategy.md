# Plotter — Brand strategy

**Status:** Working strategy for a prototype. The name is preferred, not cleared; audience motivations and market position are hypotheses to test with players.

## The idea

Plotter turns a shared patch of land into a city full of competing plans. Players choose cards, place buildings, watch the neighbourhood grow and discover that the best spot for one person might be exactly where someone else wants to build.

**Brand essence:** A little city. A lot of clever choices.

**Vision:** Make the pleasure of building a lively city something people can share in a short, memorable game.

**Mission:** Give players an inviting city, clear choices and just enough rivalry to make every placement matter.

## Positioning

For people who enjoy approachable tabletop strategy and city building, Plotter is a competitive city-building game in which everyone shapes the same board. Its card market, simultaneous building and lively toy-like city make the decisions social, spatial and quick to play.

This is a proposed position, not a claim that no other game offers these features. The strongest distinction to show is the combination: **one shared city, several competing plans**.

See [positioning](positioning.md) and the sourced [competitive landscape](competitive-landscape.md) for the reasoning and limits of this claim.

## Audience hypotheses

- **The board-game friend:** enjoys explaining a game quickly, seeing friends' plans collide and playing another round. Lead with the shared board and friendly competition.
- **The city tinkerer:** likes watching a place grow and finding satisfying neighbours for buildings. Lead with a living city and meaningful placement.
- **The solo strategist:** wants a concise puzzle with opponents and room to improve. Lead with cards, board choices and configurable AI opponents. Solo setup currently supports one to four AI opponents; multiplayer supports up to four human players.

These are segments of motivation, not researched personas. Validate them in human playtests; do not invent ages, quotes or purchase intent.

## Principles

1. **Shared by design.** Show how every player changes the same city; avoid presenting isolated personal cities as the game.
2. **Clever, not complicated.** Celebrate meaningful placement while explaining rules in plain language.
3. **Friendly rivalry.** Make competition clear without promising sabotage or hostility that the current rules do not support.
4. **A place worth looking at.** The city is a character: roads, parks and little vehicles make decisions visible and memorable.
5. **Honest delight.** Describe what exists today; treat future features, audience assumptions and match-length targets as such.

## Success to test

In playtests, ask players to explain the game in one sentence, describe their favourite placement, and say whether the city felt shared. Track how many finish a match and want to play again; record actual match times rather than turning the 8–10 minute design target into a universal promise. No target numbers are established yet.

## Story

Plotter is named for the plots players build on and the plans they make around them. Every card is an opportunity to put something into the city, but no plan exists in isolation: the next building may change the value of the one beside it. The city at the end is a record of everyone's choices.

This is a product story, not a claimed founder history.
