# Plotter — Competitive landscape

Research snapshot: 1 November 2026. These are adjacent references chosen for their overlap with city building, spatial puzzles or tabletop play. This small sample does not establish market share, audience demand, a unique market gap or legal clearance. Observations about positioning are interpretations of the cited public descriptions, not claims about player perceptions.

## Four useful comparisons

### Dorfromantik

[Toukana's description](https://www.toukana.com/dorfromantik) presents a peaceful strategy puzzle about placing tiles to create an expanding village landscape. It puts the pleasure of a picturesque landscape and relaxation first. Its [tabletop Duel](https://www.toukana.com/dorfromantik/boardgames) uses two separate landscapes.

**Lesson for Plotter:** Building an attractive place is a compelling first impression, but it does not by itself communicate Plotter's competition. Show several players contributing to the same board, then explain how the placement matters.

### ISLANDERS: New Shores

The [official site](https://playislanders.com/) presents a tranquil creative city-building experience. It combines the pleasures of an attractive miniature setting with strategic placement; its calming atmosphere is central to the pitch.

**Lesson for Plotter:** A warm setting can coexist with strategy. Plotter should show actual decisions and the changing shared board so that its competitive round structure is visible as well as its charm.

### Townscaper

The [publisher's listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1291340/Townscaper/) describes an experimental town-building toy focused on placing colourful blocks and seeing a town take shape. Its accessible creative promise is immediately legible.

**Lesson for Plotter:** A clear building silhouette can sell the pleasure of a place without a wall of explanation. Use that clarity in key art, then make the card market and player competition clear in supporting copy.

### Between Two Cities

The [publisher's description](https://store.stonemaiergames.com/products/between-two-cities-essential-edition) describes tile drafting and shared city building with neighbouring players; players balance two collaboratively built cities. It is a tabletop comparison rather than a direct digital competitor.

**Lesson for Plotter:** Shared city building is not an exclusive concept. Plotter's own story is the particular combination of a common board, a card market, simultaneous placement and individual scoring.

## Implications

The first three examples demonstrate that calm, appealing places and accessible placement are already established territory. The tabletop example demonstrates that shared city building is established too. Plotter should therefore avoid claims such as “the first shared city builder” or “unlike any other city game.”

The proposed territory is **a tabletop city with several plans in motion**. Show a coherent city, clearly distinct player ownership and a recognisable decision; use concise language to explain why a placement matters. This is a creative strategy to test, not a measured competitive moat.

## What to test

Show the visual concept and a gameplay clip to prospective players separately. Ask each group what they think players do, whether they expect cooperation or competition, and whether they can explain what makes the city shared. If they describe it as a freeform sandbox or purely cooperative game, make the market, ownership and scoring more prominent.
